The acknowledgment is painted on the current screen the instant the hold crosses the threshold and stays until the sleep screen's first waveform takes the panel, so it never flashes and needs no timer. On the reader that is the page you were reading, with the chip over it.

The sleep prep runs inside that refresh rather than in front of it: during the waveform the charge pump drives the panel with no SPI traffic and the CPU is only waiting on the busy pin, which is what Services::wave_window already exploits for background caching and housekeeping. Measured on a device the whole prep is ~750 ms, and 684 of that is the wallpaper load (session save 8, sleep card and heap drop 18, bookmark flush 9, and about 30 ms of book-record write, power log and misc) against a 654 ms DU window. The frame's duration is its write plus the longer of the two, so the acknowledgment costs about its own phase-1 write, ~122 ms, and the chip is on the panel from ~0.78 s. The sleep screen's base pass (1857 ms) and grayscale pass (386 ms) follow unchanged.

If the page is gray-coded when the hold lands, plan_partial returns RevertFirst and pays a full revert before phase 1. DEFERRED_GRAYSCALE_DELAY is 800 ms, so that is the usual case once text_aa is on, and it is off by default. Hooking the revert buys back only ~136 ms, since the prep already overlaps the DU window; the cheaper answer is to pick the waveform from the panel state, a DU when it is plain BW and a full GC when it holds gray codes, since a GC wipes them and needs no revert. Every partial drives the whole panel regardless of window (todo.md §8), so neither buys a 280×40 waveform.

Going to sleep

Held with the menu openThe menu already carries a Sleep row, so this is the one case where the chip answers a hold you started on top of a sleep affordance. The manager draws overlays after app content, so the chip wins over the sheet without any change to the sheet.

Notes

  1. Trigger. The acknowledgment is for the power-button path only: ResolvedInput::RequestSleep out of InputPolicyState::resolve on Event::LongPress(Button::Power). Idle-timeout sleep must not draw it, because nobody is there to see it. The hold fires at timing::LONG_PRESS_MS (1 s) whether or not the button is released, and the release is eaten by the policy, so the popup is not dismissible as things stand.
  2. Where it is drawn. The chip needs the UI face and the Phosphor moon, so it belongs in the distro: a widget in src/apps/widgets/ drawn by AppManager after app content and after any open overlay, the way the quick menu and the settings cache sheet are. That makes it work on every screen, not just the reader, and keeps the reader's painter untouched. If it is only ever wanted in the reader, the reader can draw it in draw_page next to POSITION_OVERLAY and save the manager a flag.
  3. Wording. Going to sleep matches the loading captions, which are all progressive (“Building pages”, “Caching chapters”). Sleeping is shorter and reads as a state rather than a process; the quick menu row is just Sleep. I would keep the progressive form for the caption in C and shorten to Sleeping if the chip feels wide.
  4. Timing. Paint it once and leave it: the sleep sequence repaints the whole panel anyway, so there is nothing to clear and no timer to run. The popup's own waveform is where the sleep prep belongs, so the dwell is automatically the prep time plus the sleep screen's base pass, and it never flashes however fast the prep is.
  5. Open questions. Should the popup say where you stopped (option B) or is naming the book the sleep screen's job, arriving a second later? And should a release during the dwell cancel the sleep? Today the gesture is one-way, and adding a cancel means a second state on the policy; I would not add it unless the hold has felt wrong in the hand.
