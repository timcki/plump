The signals reached us in fragments — never the whole transmission, always the residue. We had grown accustomed to incomplete messages, to filling the gaps with our own assumptions, but this one resisted our usual methods of interpretation.

Maya argued that we were applying the wrong framework entirely. What if the gaps were not errors of transmission but elements of the message itself? What if silence carried as much meaning as sound? She had been reading the linguists again, late into the night, marking passages with the small wooden tabs her grandmother had made.

I disagreed, but only on principle. There was something to her theory, even if she could not yet articulate why. The patterns of absence had a structure we had not anticipated. They repeated. They clustered. They behaved, in short, like a language — one in which what was missing carried more weight than what remained.

It was late October when we began the second analysis.
