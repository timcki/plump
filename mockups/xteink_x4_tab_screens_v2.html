What actually changed

  • Book titles moved to Lora. Every screen. The reader sets books in Lora and the device speaks in Atkinson; on the old three screens a book’s name was set in the device’s voice, which is the single largest reason they read as a different product from the page they lead to.
  • Every list became the sheet’s group. Home’s recent list, Stats’ book list. Both were bare rows separated by hairlines, floating on paper. They now sit in the 1 px outline with the 7 px radius that the contents sheet and the settings groups already use.
  • The captions tightened onto their groups. All three. They were centred in the gap between two groups, so they read as belonging to neither; 6 px above and 13 px below fixes the association.
  • The big-number tiles are gone from Lifetime. Stats. Three numerals at 3× body size, in a row, is a dashboard convention, and these figures are a record rather than a readout being monitored.
  • The position bar got reused. Home’s recent rows, Stats’ book list. The reader footer already earns this motif; anywhere else a number is really a fraction, the bar says so faster than the number does.
  • Values became tabular and right-aligned everywhere. All three. Columns of times and percentages now line up digit for digit.
  • Sub lines arrived on the settings rows that needed them. Settings. Ghost Clear, Text AA, Sunlight Fix and Swap Buttons cannot be understood from a label and a value, and the sheet row already had the slot.
  • Two dead screens got a bottom. Settings, Stats. An About group and a fifth book row respectively, so neither screen ends in a third of a page of blank paper.
  1. Lora is the book’s voice, Atkinson is the device’s. A book’s title, a chapter’s name and a structural entry in italics are set in Lora wherever they appear. Labels, values, captions and hints are Atkinson. No third face, and no exceptions for emphasis.
  2. Every list is the same three-column row. Lead, title with an optional sub line or bar, right-aligned tabular value, inside the outlined group. A screen that needs a list does not get to invent one.
  3. The caption belongs to the group beneath it. Tracked uppercase, 6 px above its group and 13 px below the last thing; where a count or a day helps, it sits at the caption’s right end, which is also where the sheet puts its own caption.
  4. One heavy border per screen, at most. The continue-reading card carries the 2 px ink border because it is the hero and the target of the first press. Groups are 1 px at 25% ink. Nothing else gets a border at all.
  5. The tab bar is the tab screens’ footer. Hint labels belong to sheets, which cover the bar; a tab screen leaves the bottom 64 px to the tabs and never doubles them up.
  6. An em dash, never a zero. A rate with no measurement behind it prints as —. A zero is a reading, and these screens should not report one they did not take.

As built. Shipped 2026-09-11 and flashed. Four places where the device differs from the frames above, all decided while implementing:

— Recent rows are taller. The lead cover is the cached 64×96 Mini variant, not the 48×72 the frame implies: a 1-bit dithered image cannot be resampled without destroying its dither, so the row is 104 px as it always was. The anatomy changed, the height did not.
— Stats shows five books, with the figure block. The recommended frame's Today cells over the alternate's row count: the content band had 200 px spare under three.
— The empty home card keeps its frame. It is the press target, so it stays and says where the press goes; pressing it opens the Library.
— About reads “Book cache · Battery · Awake”, and its figures are sampled when the tab opens rather than tracked. A clock that repaints itself costs a DU a minute and tells nobody anything they could not get by leaving and coming back.

What the data supports, and what it does not. Everything above reads from what is already on the card. Today’s pages and seconds come from the 16-byte DAYSTATS.BIN; the lifetime figures are the sum over _PLUMP/STATS/<filename>, which is also where the per-book sessions count and the pace come from. The time left on the home card is the sleep card’s existing pace calculation, needing 10 pages and 5 minutes of history for that book before it will print anything.

Two things need a read that is not happening yet. The percentage on a recent row is 0 in the shipped code, marked as a follow-up; it needs the bundle header’s total page count alongside the bookmark, one read per row. And Books read distinguishes books opened from books on the card, which means counting stats files rather than directory entries.

No history exists. DAYSTATS.BIN holds one day and overwrites it at rollover, so a week strip or a streak is not a styling question: it needs a new append-only file, one record a day. Worth doing on its own terms, and deliberately not designed here.
