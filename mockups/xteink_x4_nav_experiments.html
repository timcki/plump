Navigation, reconsideredThe bottom bar goes. The top bar — which is drawn on every screen already and is half empty — takes over: where you are, what a Right press gets you, one fact about this screen, and the battery.

A row of five equal slots is the touch idiom. It exists on phones because you need five tap targets of equal weight. This device has none: the bar cannot be pressed and never could. It was drawing a control that does not exist, in 64 px of an 800 px display, forever, to carry a little over two bits.

The top bar meanwhile is genuinely half empty. TopStatus::draw writes one string on the left — today · 47 pages · 1h 12m — and a battery percentage on the right, reserving the whole width in between for a line that uses about half of it. It has no border either: the code clears it to paper and stops.

So the bar that cannot be pressed goes, and the strip that was already there starts earning its keep. Four zones on one 28 px line, in the vocabulary the rest of the device already uses.

Sentence case, not a label but a name. ▶ for the next screen, ◀ on the last one where only a previous exists.

Far right on every screen, unmoved, because it is the one thing you look for rather than read.

Under the bar, at 12% ink. Neither bar has ever had an edge on any screen; both get one.

StatsSettingsUpload

The strip. Both neighbours named at the bottom. Teaches best of any option, but spends 32 px saying what the top bar can say in zero.

Settings

The ruler. The label rides its own tick, so its position is the indicator. Prettiest, and a name that lands somewhere different every time is the hardest to re-find.

spine on the edge

The spine. Five bands down the right edge. Costs width instead of height and is the one nobody understands unprompted. Still the most fun to build.

  1. The chevron names a button, not a direction on a map. ▶ means “press Right”. On Upload, where no next screen exists, the neighbour becomes the previous one and the arrow turns around rather than disappearing — so the far end of the line still tells you how to get off it.
  2. The fact is never something the screen already says. Books on the card go on Library, where the list does not total itself; books ever opened go on Stats, where it is the one figure the page below omits; the version goes on Settings. Today's reading stays on Home and nowhere else, which is also the one place it was not slightly odd.
  3. Nothing replaces the position indicator, and nothing needs to. A named neighbour implies a sequence more clearly than five dots do, and the order is five items long. There is no 4 / 5 anywhere: it would be the count problem again, telling you something the name already told you.
  4. The honest cost is a cold start. Nothing on screen says how many screens there are or that Left and Right are what move between them — you learn it in a press, but you do learn it rather than see it. That is the whole of what the 64 px was buying, and it is why the strip was the recommendation before you overruled it.
  5. Everything below moves up by 56 px. Theme::bottom_bar_h goes from 64 to 8 and content_bottom() follows, so every screen's layout arithmetic and its compile-time assertions re-derive from one constant. The reader is unaffected: it never drew the tab bar.
  6. Two things are being deleted, not moved. TabBar and Tab::icon, and with them the five Phosphor codepoints in build.rs that nothing else uses — house, books, chart-bar, gear-six and cloud-arrow-up. The icon font stays for the sheets.
