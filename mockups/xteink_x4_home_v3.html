C · Six identical rowsThe card dissolves completely. The book you are in is marked by the bookmark glyph where a percentage would sit — exactly how the contents sheet marks the chapter you are in — and its sub line carries the position instead of an author. The most books, the most consistency, and the least sense that Home is anything more than the library sorted by recency.

The content band is 700 device px: CONTENT_TOP 36 to the tab bar at 736. B and C are costed with the row as it was — 104 px, a 96 px cover with 4 px either side. The shipped row is 116 (10 px either side) and the shipped card 184, which is what takes A from 66 spare pixels to 14. Both are now compile-time assertions in home.rs, because the card had already fallen under its own cover once.

  1. Why B. It answers the thing you noticed rather than the thing you asked for. The screen was not short of pixels — it had 170 spare — it was divided into two halves by two captions and two borders, with five things in every row of the lower one. B removes a border, a caption and two row elements, and still shows one more book than A.
  2. The hero row costs almost nothing to build. The 112×160 Card cover is already cached in every bundle (it is what the card draws today), and the row already has a cover lead. The one change is letting RowLead::Cover take its box from the image instead of the fixed 64×96 constants, which is four lines.
  3. What A keeps that B gives up. A 2 px border around the current book is a stronger press target than a taller row, and "continue reading" as its own labelled thing is a clearer promise than "the first row". If Home reads to you as a launcher rather than a shelf, A is the honest choice.
  4. What C gives up. The current book's two extra figures fit only by displacing the author, and its 64×96 cover no longer stands out at a glance. The bookmark glyph is doing all the work of saying "this is the one you are in". Worth it only if the sixth book matters more than the hierarchy.
  5. The author goes in all three. It is the element paying the least rent in the list, and dropping it also drops a whole line from four rows. It stays on the current book, where there is room and where you might actually want it.
  6. The card had lost its cover entirely. CARD_INNER_H was 180 − 2×14 = 152 while the Card variant is 112×160, so the guard written to reject legacy 200×240 covers was rejecting every current one and the card has been drawing text alone. Sizing the card from the variant fixes it, and an assertion now fails the build if it ever drifts back.
  7. Nothing here needs a new cover size. Both cached variants get used as-is: Card 112×160 for the hero, Mini 64×96 for the rest. A 1-bit dithered image cannot be resampled without destroying its dither, so any layout wanting a third size would need a third variant written into every bundle.
