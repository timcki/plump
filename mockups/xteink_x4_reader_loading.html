  1. Hold before painting. No loading frame is painted until 400 ms have passed since the request, or the next step is known to be slow (chapter not cached, no layout for this font, first open). Warm opens, warm wakes and cached chapter changes therefore cost one refresh, the page itself.
  2. Paint the composition once. The plate (cover, title, author, rule, resume line) and the footer are painted together and never repainted during the open. Stage changes mark only the 40 px stage row. The title comes from the bundle header or TITLES.BIN at the first step, so it is right from the first frame; the resume line waits for the OPF parse, which is always done before a slow step.
  3. Two vocabularies, one stage row. Entering the book (open, wake, error) shows the plate. Moving inside it (chapter change, jump, font change) shows the chapter strip: text area clear, stage row above the footer, footer untouched. Both use the same caption and hairline bar, in the same place, so a slow step looks the same whichever way you arrived.
  4. Captions. Opening · Resuming · Caching chapters n of m · Typesetting chapter n of m (or the font name) · Opening page · Waiting for images (typeset held for the decode worker) · Could not open. The right-hand value is the percent, and the bar tracks it.
  5. Footer first. On the plate the footer shows the title and, when the position is known, the chapter fraction; the bar and book counter fill in when the page lands, the only footer pixels that change.

Repaint economy. Today every stage change marks the whole text area dirty, so a cold open flashes the page six or seven times. In all three options the composition above the stage row is painted once, when the title is known (the OPF parse, about a quarter of the way in; until then the previous screen stays), and later stages mark only the stage row. The footer is drawn on the loading screen already, in its final place, so when the first page lands the footer pixels do not change.
